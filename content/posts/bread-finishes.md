---
title: "Bread finishes"
date: 2025-11-23
tags: ["baking"]
---

I started baking properly during a quarter where nothing I worked on concluded.

That's not a metaphor I constructed afterwards, I noticed it at the time. Every project I touched between roughly July and October either got absorbed into a larger project, handed to another team, or is still going now. Not failures. Just things that never had a moment where you could say, right, that's done.

Bread has that moment. It is extremely obvious. It is a loud crack when the crust cools, and it happens about four hours after you decided to make bread, and you can hear it from the next room.

The loop is short and the feedback is honest. Overproof it and it spreads out flat and you know exactly why. Underproof it and you get a dense seam near the base, and you know why that is too. Nobody is going to tell you the loaf was directionally correct or that it's a good foundation for the next loaf. It's just bread, and you eat it, and then it's gone and you make another one.

I do a fairly boring routine now. Feed the starter Friday evening, mix Saturday morning, four sets of folds, shape in the afternoon, then it sits in the fridge overnight and goes into a very hot cast iron pot on Sunday. The overnight bit in the fridge is doing most of the work and I did not know that for about a year, I thought the folding was the clever part. The folding is barely anything. It's mostly time and temperature, and I was fussing over the one variable I could perform.

Which I did recognise as familiar, once I saw it.

There's a similar thing with running, where the useful part is going out three times a week for a year and the not useful part is optimising your shoes, but running takes months to tell you anything. Bread tells you on Sunday afternoon.

Last weekend's was underproofed. Cold kitchen, I didn't adjust, straight from the fridge into the oven while it was still too firm. Dense at the bottom, fine at the top, perfectly edible and slightly disappointing.

I'll do another one on Saturday.
