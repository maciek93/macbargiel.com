---
title: "Everything is a progress bar"
date: 2026-05-17
tags: ["games", "rationality"]
---

I quit a game last year in the middle of a Tuesday evening, not because it had got worse but because I noticed I was doing my dailies.

Three of them. About twenty five minutes. I wasn't enjoying it, I was clearing it, the way you clear an inbox. And I'd been doing that for something like five months without once asking whether I wanted to.

The design is not subtle once you look at it directly. There are daily objectives that expire, so missing them feels like losing something rather than not gaining something, and people will work considerably harder to avoid a loss. There's a battle pass with a bar that fills, which you paid for, which means stopping now wastes what you already put in, which is not how sunk costs actually work but is exactly how they feel. There are limited time events, so scarcity does the rest. And underneath all of it the rewards arrive on a schedule that is deliberately irregular, because predictable rewards are much easier to walk away from than unpredictable ones.

I knew all of this. I have read the papers, I find this stuff genuinely interesting, I would have happily explained the whole mechanism to you at a dinner party. Made no difference whatsoever. Knowing the name of the thing that is working on you does not stop it working on you, it just means you can narrate it while it happens.

The annoying part is that once you've seen the shape you see it constantly.

My running app has a streak. It is very keen that I know about the streak. There was a week in February where I went out in genuinely unpleasant weather, and I'd like to say that was discipline, but I'd checked the number first. The number was 31. It is not obvious to me that the app and I want the same thing, since the app wants me to open the app.

And then, unavoidably, work.

Nobody at work is being cynical about this. That's what makes it awkward. Somebody built a dashboard where the quarter's objectives show a completion percentage, because a bar that fills is motivating and everyone knows it's motivating, and there's no villain in the story. But the bar has to be filled by something countable, and so the countable things become the work. I have watched a team get to 90% on a perfectly reasonable set of objectives while the thing the objectives were meant to achieve got quietly worse, and everyone involved was working hard and reporting honestly.

The bar was accurate. It was just measuring the wrong film.

I don't have a clean position on this. Progress bars work, that's the whole problem, and a team with no visible sense of movement is genuinely worse to be on than a team with a slightly wrong metric. I've tried running a quarter on vibes and narrative alone and it was not better, people found it unmoored and kept asking where they stood.

What I've mostly ended up preferring, in games at least, is things that end. Outer Wilds ends. Slay the Spire ends every twenty minutes and then you decide, deliberately, with a clear head, whether to start again. There's no version of it that's disappointed in you on Thursday for not showing up. The good ones ask for your attention once and then give it back.

Anyway. I still have the running streak. It's at 61 and I check it more than I'd like to admit, and I have decided I'm fine with that one, on the basis that the incentive and the thing I actually want happen to point the same direction at the moment.

Ask me again if it gets to 200 and it's raining.
